## How It Works

Moon & Mars Base Survival is a browser-based strategy game for students learning about space exploration.

Players begin by choosing the Moon or Mars, followed by a difficulty level: Cadet or Commander. They control a fictional four-person outpost over ten game days.

The base has four resource meters: Power, Oxygen, Food, and Shield. Players operate solar arrays, oxygen generators, greenhouses, and other equipment. Each action consumes time or resources. Daily operating costs are deducted when the player ends the day.

Mission events introduce new challenges. On Mars, players encounter dust storms and habitat pressure warnings. Lunar missions include solar particle events and micrometeorite impacts. Each event presents an illustration and two possible responses. Players can review the reasons behind each resource change before making a decision.

## Science Activities

Players can earn Science badges by completing research tasks.

The lunar mission introduces radiation monitoring, water-ice exploration, and lunar surface research.

The Martian mission introduces atmospheric observations, geological studies, and plant growth experiments.

These activities reflect research topics explored in NASA publications. The in-game NASA Science panel provides links to relevant sources.

## Learning Outcomes

The game encourages students to examine resource trade-offs. Producing oxygen requires electricity. Growing food also consumes power. Research activities provide additional rewards while using resources needed for survival.

Players receive a Bronze, Silver, or Gold performance medal based on their science badges and remaining resources. Their chosen difficulty is displayed separately.

## Development Tools

The project uses HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. It runs locally in a web browser and requires no installation.

AI tools assisted with coding, interface design, game parameters, explanatory text, and original illustrations. Browser-generated audio provides optional environmental sounds and mission alerts.

NASA publications informed the scientific topics. Resource values use a simplified 0–100 game scale designed for classroom play.

## Design Considerations

The interface places interactive controls directly over the corresponding equipment. Event illustrations help younger students understand unfamiliar hazards.

The game includes two planetary environments, adjustable difficulty, and explanations of event consequences. Its short mission format allows students to try different strategies and compare their outcomes.
